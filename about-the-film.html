ABOUT THE FILM

Recognising Australian Veterans as heroes.

From an 8-minute TV segment to a feature documentary

DARWIN 1942 — THE FILM

This documentary began as an ANZAC Day TV segment broadcast across the Nine Network in 2026, telling the story of celebrated WW2 Veteran Wilbert 'Darky' Hudson and a commemorative lunch hosted by Merrylands RSL Club.

The response was overwhelming. RSL Clubs and Sub Branches replayed the segment at ANZAC Day services, and requests to rerun the story kept coming. After meeting with Merrylands RSL Club and the Hudson family, the concept grew into a feature documentary – told from the perspective of the Veterans who fought to defend Australia.

Darwin 1942: the forgotten war that reshaped Australia is the documentary which commemorates the 85th anniversary of the WW2 bombing of Darwin. It tells the story of the largest military attack on Australian soil since Federation from the perspective of the Veterans who defended Australia.

More bombs were dropped on Darwin during WW2 than on Pearl Harbor. The Fall of Singapore ended British dominance in the Pacific. The Allies were losing the war … and Australia was next.

During the 21-months of bombing raids, countless attacks along our coastlines, battles in Broome, Townsville, and the Coral Sea, and military activity in Sydney Harbour, the Mornington Peninsula, Gippsland, Fremantle and along the West Australian coastline, Bass Strait and the Great Australian Bight, Australia had a weakened defence force and prepared itself for invasion.

"Darwin 1942 will bring to life the stories of our WW2 heroes who fought on Australian soil."
— Richard Attieh, Producer and Director

Drawing on Veteran stories identified by RSL Sub Branches and Clubs, passed onto their children, personal family heirlooms, private photo collections, rare archival footage, and recordings of Veteran testimonies, the film brings this largely forgotten story to life.

Darwin 1942 is the story that should have been told decades ago.

Lest We Forget.

Production

MAKING THE FILM

The production of Darwin 1942 is driven by a deep sense of urgency. The stories of our Veterans, their experiences, and their voices are being forgotten. Once lost, they will be gone forever. If this story is going to be told with the authenticity it deserves, the time to tell it is now.

ARCHIVAL RESEARCH

The production team is combing through national archives, military and RSL Sub Branch records, and private family collections to source stories, footage, photographs, and documents that have never been seen by the public. Darwin 1942 will provide access to never before seen footage.

VETERAN TESTIMONIES

At the heart of the film are the stories of the Veterans who defended Australia. Archival interviews, stories passed onto children, photos shared on social media, and responses from the families of those who have since passed form the emotional backbone of Darwin 1942 – giving the history a human face.

CINEMATIC APPROACH

Darwin 1942 is made with the same care and craft as the finest theatrical documentaries. It is built for the big screen and designed to move audiences. Every frame, every sound, every interview and every Veteran story is selected with that intention.

COMMUNITY PARTNERSHIP

The film is being produced in partnership with RSL Sub Branches, Clubs, military historical societies, community groups and Veteran organisations who recognise the significance of commemorating this event. Their involvement helps to identify Veteran stories, and ensures authenticity, accuracy, awareness, and community engagement with this film.

Distribution

BRINGING THIS FILM TO AUSTRALIA

Darwin 1942 will be distributed through Heritage Films – Australia's leading film distribution company for community and event screenings. The film will be available for cinematic release and streaming, as well as community screenings at RSL Sub Branches, Clubs, museums, military historical societies, cultural institutions, schools and public venues across the country.

Our goal is simple: every Australian should have the opportunity to see this film and hear these stories. We are committed to making Darwin 1942 as accessible as possible – in major cities, small towns, regional areas and in the communities where this history lives most vividly.
